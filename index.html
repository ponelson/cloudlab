Same model, different door.

A frontier lab sells its models and the product surface it builds around them. A cloud model platform sells many labs' models inside the cloud's identity, network, billing and compliance perimeter. In 2026 the old exclusivities broke: Claude runs on all three hyperscalers, and since 28 April OpenAI's frontier models run on AWS as well as Azure. The choice now turns on who operates inference, who processes the prompt, which features and safeguards come with the model, where agent state lives, and whose contract you sign. Each door is also a deal: the clouds that host the labs own parts of them, sell them compute and collect their bills.

00 · How to read the claims

Six ways "we have Claude" misleads.

Most vendor statements in this market are true and still answer the wrong question. These six traps are worth learning before the tables, because every table below is built to avoid them.

1 · Catalog is not operator

A model listed in a cloud console can be run by the cloud, by the model maker behind the cloud's front door, or by a third party the cloud routes to. The listing does not say which. Section 02 sorts paths by operator.

2 · Infrastructure is not operator

"Hosted on Azure" can mean Anthropic runs the service on Azure hardware, acting as an independent processor for Microsoft S36. Where the servers sit and who operates them are separate facts, and contracts need both.

3 · Same name, different service

One set of weights now ships with different safeguards, retention rules, access tiers and serving hardware depending on the path S52 S50. Section 05 lists what varies.

4 · List parity is not cost parity

Rates match at list. Regional endpoint premiums, batch eligibility, caching and commit treatment do not S49 S23. The calculator in section 04 prices a workload rather than a rate card.

5 · The door covers the call, not the turn

An agent turn can invoke search, a code sandbox, a file store or an MCP server (open source), each with its own operator S48. Section 06 extends the checklist from models to tools.

6 · Channel revenue is not customer revenue

A lab can book a cloud-collected sale as its own gross revenue and record the cloud's cut as a marketing expense S44. Read lab revenue and cloud AI revenue with the channel in mind.

Keys used throughout

Doors. 1 maker direct · 2 maker-run, platform-billed · 3 platform-operated. Amber marks independent inference clouds; grey marks data platforms, gateways, applications and regional platforms.

Source tiers. A filing, vendor documentation or primary announcement · B wire service, major outlet, trade analyst or named research house · C secondary, single-source or third-party analysis, directional only. Tiers are shown in the source list in section 15.

Marked analysis. Passages that open with "Reading" or sit under "Judgments" are this note's interpretation, not a vendor's statement.

01 · Two businesses

A lab sells a model and its product. A cloud sells a perimeter.

The model can be identical on both paths. What differs is everything wrapped around the call.

A frontier lab (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Mistral, xAI) earns its margin on the model and on the product surface closest to it: the native API where new tools, agent runtimes and beta features ship first, first-party applications used by employees, and increasingly an agent-management platform that competes for the same enterprise budget as the clouds. The lab also sells a relationship: roadmap visibility, direct escalation, and in OpenAI's case forward-deployed engineers attached to its Frontier agent platform S17.

A cloud model platform (Amazon Bedrock, Microsoft Foundry, Google's Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, formerly Vertex AI) earns its margin on keeping the workload inside the cloud account. It offers a multi-lab catalog behind one API family, cloud-operated inference in chosen regions, IAM and private networking, content guardrails, retrieval and agent runtimes, audit logging into the cloud's own tools, and a line item that can draw down an existing spend commitment. The model is one input to a governance and procurement argument.

Lab direct

What a frontier lab sells

Models on the lab's own serving stack, with the newest capabilities on day one.

Platform features that reach the lab API first and some cloud doors late or not at all: managed agents, skills, code execution, files, native web search and fetch S48 S73.

Applications: ChatGPT Enterprise and ChatGPT Work, Codex; Claude Enterprise, Claude Code, Claude Cowork; the Gemini app and Google AI Studio.

Agent platforms: OpenAI Frontier (launched 5 February 2026), Claude Managed Agents S18 S3.

Cloud-operated

What a cloud model platform sells

Catalog breadth: several labs plus open-weight models under one set of APIs and one console.

Operator control: inference on cloud-controlled infrastructure, with regional endpoints for residency.

Governance: IAM, private endpoints, guardrails, audit trails, and compliance programs that cover in-scope models, regions and configurations, not the whole catalog by default.

Commercials: consolidated billing and commit drawdown, at base list-price parity with the labs' first-party rates for the major proprietary models S12 S6.

Two hyperscalers are also labs. Google builds Gemini and hosts Claude beside it; Microsoft added seven first-party MAI models to Foundry after Build 2026, in private preview, while hosting OpenAI, Anthropic and others S11. Amazon lists its own Nova family in Bedrock S22. Every cloud is therefore a distributor for the labs it hosts and a competitor to them, and Amazon, Google and Microsoft are also shareholders in the labs they distribute (section 04). That combination explains most of the channel behavior described below.

02 · The axis that matters

Ask who runs inference, not who lists the model.

The old "cloud versus direct" choice now has three doors. Each is defined by who operates inference relative to who made the model, so the doors apply to any provider type, not only labs and hyperscalers.

Door 2 is the one that trips procurement. Claude Platform on AWS, launched 11 May 2026, is reached with AWS credentials, logged in CloudTrail and billed through AWS Marketplace, but Anthropic operates it and processes the data outside the AWS boundary S2 S4. AWS's own documentation calls it a third-party offering not covered by AWS compliance programs, and points customers who need AWS as sole processor to Bedrock S1. Claude in Microsoft Foundry has the same operator in both of its hosting options: Anthropic runs the service, on Anthropic infrastructure or on Azure infrastructure, and acts as an independent processor for Microsoft S36. The Azure-hosted option keeps prompts and completions in Azure, with only usage metadata and content flagged by Anthropic's safety systems leaving it S36, which makes it the one Door 2 path whose data location resembles Door 3. A buyer who hears "Claude, on our AWS bill" or "Claude, hosted on Azure" can land on either side of the operator line.

Door 3 has tightened. Anthropic documents Claude in Amazon Bedrock as running on AWS-managed infrastructure with zero operator access for Anthropic personnel S49, and AWS describes OpenAI models on Bedrock as running with in-region processing and hardware-enforced isolation from operators S13. Anthropic labels Claude on Google Cloud as Google-operated, the second platform-run Claude path S48. On Azure, Claude went generally available on 29 June 2026 with an Azure-hosted option and usage counting toward Azure consumption commitments for eligible customers S9 S10; v1.1 placed that option in Door 3, and this version moves it to Door 2 because Anthropic, not Microsoft, operates it S36.

03 · Why Door 2 exists

Labs ship to their own surface first.

Clouds get the major models at base list-price parity. The agent features around the model arrive later, or as a cloud-native equivalent instead.

Per-token list rates for Claude are the same across the three AWS doors S6, and AWS states that OpenAI pricing on Bedrock matches OpenAI's first-party rates S12. List price is therefore not the differentiator. Timing and feature fidelity are. v1.1 relied on a May 2026 third-party comparison for the gap S7; Anthropic's own feature matrix, re-read for this version on 12 October 2026, shows it persisting five months later. Claude Managed Agents is offered only on the direct API and Claude Platform on AWS S73. Code execution, web fetch, Agent Skills and the Files API run on the API, Claude Platform on AWS and Foundry's Anthropic-hosted option; web search adds Google Cloud; Bedrock's Messages endpoint carries none of the server-side tools S48 S49. OpenAI made the opposite design choice on AWS: it is building a Stateful Runtime Environment with Amazon that is served through Bedrock S38, while its models on Bedrock still come through two endpoints with different feature coverage S15.

That lag is the commercial reason Door 2 exists. The lab keeps control of its feature surface and customer relationship while accepting the cloud's front door and bill. The cloud accepts it because the alternative is the customer leaving the account to go direct.

List price is not effective cost

Parity holds at the base list price. Effective cost still diverges on terms that sit outside that number. Bedrock alone lists Standard, Flex, Priority and Reserved service tiers, batch pricing 50% below on-demand for select models, and separate pricing for global cross-region versus in-region inference S23. Prompt-caching discounts (90% on cached input for OpenAI models on Bedrock) S13, provisioned throughput, marketplace fee treatment, support plans and network charges also differ by door. Bedrock's regional endpoints for Claude add 10% over global ones S49. Compare on a modeled workload, not a rate card; the calculator in section 04 does that.

04 · Follow the money

Every door is also a deal.

The clouds that host a lab's models also own part of it, sell it compute, collect its bills and compete with it. The doors in section 02 follow the contracts below.

v1.1 of this note explained the end of exclusivity with a compute purchase. The fuller sequence runs through equity and a license. OpenAI's October 2025 recapitalization left Microsoft with exclusive rights to OpenAI's IP and models until an AGI determination, while dropping Microsoft's right of first refusal on compute in return for a $250B Azure commitment S74. On 27 February 2026 Amazon agreed to invest $50B in OpenAI, $15B at once and $35B on conditions, and AWS became the exclusive third-party cloud distributor for OpenAI Frontier S38. Azure still held exclusivity over OpenAI's stateless APIs at that point S72. On 27 April Microsoft's license became non-exclusive and OpenAI gained the right to serve all its products on any cloud S37. GPT-5.5 reached Bedrock the next day S16.

Reading. The $38B compute contract of November 2025 bought capacity. The distribution right came from a shareholder relationship and a renegotiated license. Anthropic's AWS path ran the same way: Claude Platform on AWS, the first mainstream Door 2, was announced inside the April 2026 deal in which Amazon added $5B of equity and Anthropic committed more than $100B to AWS over ten years S40.

Where a dollar of Claude spend goes, by door

At list-price parity the buyer pays the same on every door. The seller does not keep the same amount. Reuters, reviewing Anthropic's confidential IPO filing, reported that 47% of its 2025 revenue, about $2.16B, came through the AWS and Google cloud marketplaces, and calculated that Anthropic paid about $351M back in distribution fees, roughly 16 cents per dollar of those sales S44. The channel share rose from 11% of revenue in 2023 to 32% in 2024 and 47% in 2025 S44. Anthropic books those sales as gross revenue, as principal, and records the platforms' cut under sales, marketing and partnerships S44. A secondary account of the same filing says the two clouds also held about 60% of Anthropic's $909M of outstanding customer receivables at the end of 2025, and that the filing itself names the arrangement as a source of potential conflicts of interest S45.

The fifth role: shareholder

The same clouds also mark up their stakes. Amazon's first-quarter 2026 net income included $16.8B of pre-tax gains from its Anthropic investments, more than half of its pre-tax income for the quarter, and Fortune reported that about $28.7B of Alphabet's record quarterly profit came from outside its products, a gain it tied to the same investment S43. Amazon's second-quarter 10-Q records about $62.8B of upward adjustments to its Anthropic preferred stock for the first half of 2026 and puts the fair value of its remaining Anthropic notes at about $97.9B S42.

Reading. A hyperscaler can be paid on all three doors and on the lab's valuation at once: inference margin on Door 3, a fee on Door 2, compute rent when the lab sells on Door 1, and the equity mark underneath all of it. That is why clouds tolerate Door 2 and why they have little reason to close any door.

The split nobody publishes

No lab or cloud discloses revenue by door. The Reuters figures separate cloud-channel sales from direct sales but not Bedrock from Claude Platform on AWS, nor Foundry's two hosting options from each other S44. The report that AWS takes about half of Anthropic's gross profit on AWS sales is single-sourced S46. Treat every per-door margin claim in circulation as an estimate.

05 · Same model?

Same weights, not always the same service.

The headline of this note treats the model as the constant. In 2026 serving hardware, safeguards, retention, capacity and availability all vary by door, and sometimes by hosting option inside one door.

The door picks the hardware, and the hardware has its own failure modes

Anthropic serves Claude through its own API, Bedrock and Google Cloud on three hardware platforms: AWS Trainium, NVIDIA GPUs and Google TPUs S50. Its September 2025 postmortem shows what that means for a buyer. A routing error that sent short-context requests to servers configured for a 1M-token window reached 16% of Sonnet 4 requests in its worst hour, while misrouted traffic peaked at 0.18% of Sonnet 4 requests on Bedrock and stayed below 0.0004% on Vertex AI. A separate misconfiguration corrupted output on the Claude API's own TPU servers. The routing fix reached the first-party API and Vertex by 16 September and Bedrock by 18 September S50. Same model, three exposure profiles, two fix dates.

One set of weights, several safeguard and access regimes

Anthropic states that Claude Fable 5 and Fable 5.1 share the same underlying model as Claude Mythos 5 and Mythos 5.1, with additional safeguards in the cyber and bio domains S52. On Bedrock, Fable models are open to all customers, Mythos 5 and 5.1 require verification, and Claude Mythos Preview is invitation-only through Project Glasswing, served from us-east-1 to an allowlisted, dedicated AWS account S49. Foundry carries Fable and Mythos only in its Anthropic-hosted option, with Mythos Preview open to invited customers S36. The door now decides which safety configuration of a model you can reach, not only who runs it.

Retention follows the model; where it is held follows the door

For covered models, the Mythos class and successors Anthropic designates, prompts and outputs are retained for 30 days on every platform where the models are offered, effective 9 June 2026. Organizations with zero data retention must turn retention on to use them unless Anthropic notifies them that they are eligible to keep ZDR S52. Where the retained data sits differs by door: with Anthropic for the Claude API and Claude Platform on AWS, inside AWS for Bedrock, inside Google Cloud for Agent Platform, and in a separate Azure subscription for Foundry customers whose main subscription is configured for ZDR S52. A contract that says "zero data retention" without naming the model is incomplete.

Capacity and quotas are per door

Claude Platform on AWS draws on a capacity pool separate from both the first-party API and Bedrock S3. Bedrock's Messages endpoint defaults to 2 million input tokens per minute and allows up to 5 million input and 500,000 output tokens per minute without Anthropic's approval, with request-rate limits set by AWS S49. Foundry responses omit Anthropic's rate-limit headers; limits are managed in Azure S36. A launch-week capacity squeeze on one door says little about the others.

Availability is a door attribute too

On 12 June 2026 Anthropic received a US export-control directive to suspend access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 by any foreign national. Anthropic said the practical effect was that it had to disable both models for all customers, while access to its other models was unaffected; it complied and stated that it disagreed with the basis for the directive S51. Mythos 5 access was restored to a set of vetted US organizations on 26 June, the Commerce Department lifted the controls on 30 June, and Fable 5 returned on Anthropic's own surfaces on 1 July, with the cloud platforms to follow S53. A hands-on test found Bedrock serving Fable 5 again on 1 July through its US cross-region profile, with the global profile not yet responding S54.

Reading

The suspension reached every door at once; the restoration did not. A buyer with a tested second path to the same model could resume on whichever door returned first. Resilience is now a reason to hold more than one door, alongside cost and features.

06 · Tools and agent state

The door covers the call, not the turn.

An agent turn is a model call plus the tools it invokes, the files it reads and the state it keeps. Each has an operator, and it need not be the model's.

On the doors that carry the maker's server-side tools, the maker runs them: web search and fetch, code execution, Agent Skills, the Files API and the MCP connector all run on Anthropic's side on the Claude API and Claude Platform on AWS S48. On Bedrock's Messages endpoint none of them run S49, so an agent built there supplies its own search, sandbox and connectors, each with its own operator, region and terms. Foundry's Azure-hosted option sits in between: only the basic versions of web search and fetch, and requests for code execution, Skills or Files return an error by design S36.

Retention can change with the feature on the same door. Anthropic lists code execution, Agent Skills, the MCP connector, the Files API and batch processing as not eligible for zero data retention, while prompt caching, the memory tool and web search without dynamic filtering are eligible S48. Switching on a tool can take a workload out of a ZDR arrangement without changing the door.

Reading. A cached prompt prefix is held by whichever party operates inference for the life of the cache, five minutes or an hour S48. "Prompts stay in the perimeter" has a time dimension as well as a location.

Where agent state lives is the next door decision

Claude Managed Agents, Anthropic's hosted agent runtime, is available only on the direct API and Claude Platform on AWS S73, so sessions and agent memory on that runtime sit with Anthropic even when the bill is AWS's. OpenAI took the other route on AWS: its Stateful Runtime Environment is being built jointly with Amazon and served through Bedrock S38, and Bedrock's managed agents are now described as powered by OpenAI S70. Two labs, one cloud, opposite answers to where an agent's state is kept.

Reading. Models are replaced every few months; an agent's accumulated state (memory, files, traces, tool credentials) is not. The door question is moving from "who runs the model" to "who holds the state the model works on." World Models makes the same argument for physical AI, where the durable world-state store is the role nobody yet owns; Agentic Memory 2026 covers the enterprise version.

Release windows

Reading. A studio sells a film first where it keeps the most margin and controls the experience, then licenses it down a sequence of windows (the mechanics are in Hollywood Accounting). Labs now do the same with capabilities: features ship on the maker's API, then on the maker-run cloud door, then later or never on the cloud-operated door (section 03). The gap between doors is not a lag waiting to be closed. It is the release strategy, and its length is a negotiated term. The July 2026 restoration ran in the same order: the maker's own surfaces first, the clouds after (section 05).

Door 2 has a music-industry analogue as well: a label that keeps its catalog and its artists but ships through a major's distribution and billing (the structure is in Music). The lab keeps the product and the customer relationship; the cloud supplies the storefront, the identity system and the invoice.

Licenses and contracts as door control

Reading. Open-source vendors who could not control the doors through distribution contracts used licenses instead: Elastic's SSPL, and Meta's 700-million-user clause aimed squarely at the largest platforms. Proprietary labs control their doors through distribution agreements and equity, which is why section 04 says more about where a model will be available next year than any catalog page does.

10 · Doors are not provider types

The two classifications are orthogonal.

The doors describe a delivery arrangement for one model on one path. The five categories describe businesses. Most businesses sit behind more than one door.

Reading the landscape table as a list of doors produces errors. Snowflake is a data platform, but a Claude call from Cortex is served on cloud-provider infrastructure, not on Snowflake GPUs S26. OpenRouter is a gateway, so it adds no door of its own: the door is whichever upstream provider served that request, and by default that can change between requests S31. The point

The catalog is close to a commodity. The operator is not.

Two paths to the same model can differ on all thirteen fields above, and in 2026 the path also decides who gets paid and where agent state lives. Treat each model path as its own procurement object, and make the thirteen fields part of the order form rather than the sales conversation.

13 · Falsifiers and judgments

What would break this, and three calls.

The thesis is testable. These are the conditions under which it fails, followed by judgments labeled as judgments, with dates to check them against.

What would falsify "the catalog is a commodity, the operator is not"

Day-one parity on a cloud-operated door. If Bedrock or Google Cloud carries a maker's full server-side tool set and agent runtime on launch day for a whole model generation, Door 2 loses its feature reason and the operator matters only for compliance.

A lab pulls a frontier model from a cloud door, or a cloud drops a lab. That would show the door is a lever in a negotiation rather than a settled channel, and that equity ties do not guarantee distribution.

Door 2 enters the platform's compliance programs. If AWS or Microsoft brings a maker-run service inside its own attestations, Doors 2 and 3 converge for regulated buyers and the distinction this note draws stops mattering to them.

Channel economics converge. If a lab's public filings show cloud-channel margins in line with direct margins, the commercial asymmetry in section 04 was a phase, not a structure.

Judgment

Procurement disputes move to the operator line

Most model-access contract fights in 2027 will turn on processor, retention location and tool operators rather than price. Those are already the fields vendors answer least precisely.

Judgment

Agent state becomes the binding door decision

The hardest choice to reverse will be where agent memory, files and runtime live, because models are swapped and state is not. Section 06 shows the two leading labs already choosing differently on the same cloud.

Judgment

Clouds keep every door open

A cloud paid inference margin on Door 3, a fee on Door 2, compute rent on Door 1 and an equity mark on the lab has little reason to close any of them. Expect more maker-run doors on clouds, not fewer.
